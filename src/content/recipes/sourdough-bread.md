## Instructions

1. Premix (fermentolyse), about 1 hour.
   - Mix the starter and water together first, then add the flour and salt.
   - Combine into a shaggy dough, cover, and rest for about 1 hour.
2. Form the dough, then rest 30 minutes.
   - Work the dough around the bowl, folding the exterior over the center until it forms a smooth ball.
   - Cover and rest for 30 minutes.
3. Stretch and fold, 4 to 6 sets over about 2 hours.
   - Each set is 4 stretches and folds, with about 15 minutes resting between sets.
   - This builds gluten structure without kneading.
4. Bulk ferment until doubled.
   - Cover and leave at room temperature until the dough is smooth, pillowy, and roughly doubled, with bubbles visible underneath.
   - Watch the dough, not the clock — timing varies a lot with room temperature.
5. Shape.
   - Turn the dough onto the counter and shape into a boule or batard, building surface tension.
   - Place seam-side up in a floured banneton.
6. Cold ferment, 5 to 36 hours.
   - Cover loosely and refrigerate.
   - Longer cold ferments develop more flavor and better blistering on the crust.
7. Bake.
   - Preheat the oven to 450°F (230°C) with a Dutch oven inside.
   - Turn the cold dough onto parchment paper and score it.
   - Bake covered for 30 minutes, then uncovered at 410°F (210°C) for 10 to 15 minutes, until deeply golden.
8. Cool completely before slicing, 4 to 6 hours.
   - Slicing too early ruins the crumb structure.
