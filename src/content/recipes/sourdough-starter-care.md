## Counter feeding (1:1:1)

1. Weigh out the starter into a clean jar; discard (or set aside for baking) the rest.
   - Add the water first, then the starter, then the flour — it mixes more evenly in that order.
2. Stir vigorously until no dry flour remains, scrape down the sides, and loosely cover the jar. It needs to vent, not seal.
3. Leave at room temperature until it peaks — roughly doubled, domed, and full of bubbles.
   - A rubber band around the jar at the starting level makes it easy to track.
4. Use at peak, or feed again every 12 to 24 hours if baking 3 to 4+ times a week.

## Fridge feeding (1:2:2)

1. Before refrigerating, feed at a heavier ratio — for example 25g starter to 50g flour and 50g water.
2. Let it sit out until active (bubbly, starting to rise), then move it to the fridge.
3. Feed weekly. Pull it out, let it come to room temperature, feed, wait for activity, then either bake or return it to the fridge.

## Is it ready to use?

- Doubled, or close to it, since feeding
- Visibly bubbly throughout, not just on top
- Domed or rounded top, not yet collapsed
- Smells pleasantly sour and yeasty, not sharp

## Troubleshooting

- **Hooch (dark liquid on top):** Starter is hungry. Stir it back in for a more sour loaf, or pour it off and feed right away.
- **Sharp, acetone smell:** Overdue for a feeding — feed at 1:1:1 and it should bounce back within a feeding or two.
- **White film (kahm yeast):** Scrape it off carefully, then feed as usual.
- **Any pink, orange, or fuzzy mold:** Discard the whole thing and start over — don't try to save it.

## Notes

- Weigh everything on a digital scale — volume measurements aren't consistent enough for a starter.
- New starters need daily feeding for 3 to 4 months before they're established enough to move to the fridge.
- Discard from a starter that's at least a week old is safe to use in recipes that call for it.
